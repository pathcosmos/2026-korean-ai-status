Items where the number came from a secondary summary rather than the primary page are tagged **[secondary]**. Items tagged **[vendor]** are self-reported by the company that sells the product. ## 1. Domain shift across machines / plants / operating conditions (fault diagnosis, PdM, process and quality models)

### Takeaway
The literature consistently reports that models trained on one machine or dataset lose a large share of accuracy on another (roughly 50% vs 86% in one cross-dataset comparison), and that published headline accuracies on the standard bearing benchmarks are inflated by data leakage. Recovery with domain adaptation or a small labelled target set is real but uneven: some transfer pairs are "saturated", others (notably transfers involving the Paderborn dataset) stay in the mid-80s even with 10% target labels.

### Cited Findings
- Cross-dataset bearing diagnosis (train on one of CWRU / IMS / a railway locomotive set, test on another): a conventional CNN averaged 53.1% accuracy, a domain-adaptation method 86.3%. Reported in a review first posted in 2019 **[older]** — [Zhang et al., "Machine Learning and Deep Learning Algorithms for Bearing Fault Diagnostics – A Comprehensive Review", arXiv 1901.08247](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.08247)
- A 2026 arXiv paper using an LLM-based two-stage transformer reports 92.61% accuracy with only 10% labelled target data, but results on the Paderborn (PU) dataset remain around 85–87% for both compared methods; the authors attribute this to differing sampling rates, fault taxonomies and bearing geometry — [An LLM-based Two-Stage Transformer Framework for Cross-Domain Bearing Fault Diagnosis with Limited Data, arXiv 2606.24459 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.24459)
- A PHM Society (PHME) paper by Knap, Jachymczyk and Lalik (AGH Krakow) defines a leakage-safe benchmark with six fixed source→target scenarios on CWRU and Paderborn and reports that some transfer settings are already saturated while others remain much harder; code public at github.com/1Sensor/pdm-bench — [Leakage-Safe, Reproducible Benchmarking for Vibration-Based Fault Diagnosis, PHM Society European Conference](https://papers.phmsociety.org/index.php/phme/article/view/4924)
- Vieira et al. (Federal University of Santa Catarina, Sep 2025) argue that data leakage makes bearing fault results look better than they are and propose bearing-wise partitioning (no physical bearing shared between train and test). A third-party summary reports CWRU reaching 99.8–100% AUROC under segment-level or bearing-level leakage **[secondary]** — [Towards a more realistic evaluation of machine learning models for bearing fault diagnosis, arXiv 2509.22267 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22267v1)
- A 2026 CMES paper (Alsafari and Yafoz) defines a "double domain shift" (fault severity and load both change between train and test), benchmarks six few-shot architectures on CWRU and PU, and notes that the overwhelming majority of bearing-diagnosis papers report only accuracy, which overstates performance when per-class recall is unequal — [CMES vol. 148 no. 1 (2026)](https://www.techscience.com/CMES/v148n1/68217)
- Anecdotal practitioner report (blog, not peer reviewed; the URL slug says 89%→67%, the search excerpt says 97%→67%): a CWRU-trained classifier dropped to 67% on a pump manufacturer's custom bearings. Use only as illustration — [DEV Community post](https://dev.to/tildalice/transfer-learning-cwru-to-custom-bearings-89-to-67-drop-fix-2dd6)
- Process industry soft sensors: the standard survey is Curreri, Patanè and Xibilia, "Soft Sensor Transferability: A Survey", Applied Sciences 2021 **[older]** — [MDPI Applied Sciences 11(16):7710](https://mdpi.com/2076-3417/11/16/7710)
- Negative transfer is treated as a central risk in soft-sensor transfer learning: Yamada and Kaneko note it arises when the source domain is unrelated to the target or source training interferes with target training, and that there are no criteria for deciding when to stop transfer learning — [Adaptive soft sensor based on transfer learning and ensemble learning for multiple process states (PMC10989538)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10989538/)
- Structural barrier to cross-plant transfer: "the fixed input structure of standard soft sensor models prohibits transfer if, e.g., the sensor information is not identical in all plants"; the paper tests topology-aware graph neural networks across ammonia synthesis loops with different topologies — [Transferring Graph Neural Networks for Soft Sensor Modeling using Process Topologies, arXiv 2502.06826 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06826v1)

### Inferences
- The size of the cross-machine drop is benchmark-dependent and not comparable across papers; the defensible general statement is "tens of percentage points without adaptation, partly recoverable with adaptation or a small labelled target set".
- Because the two most-used public bearing datasets are laboratory test rigs and are affected by leakage, academic "99%" results say little about plant-level transferability; the leakage-safe benchmarks (2025–2026) are the more relevant evidence.
- For process and quality models the obstacle is often structural (different sensor sets and plant topology), which fine-tuning alone does not solve; this is a data-schema problem before it is a modelling problem.

### Gaps
- No quantified cross-plant results were found for process optimisation or quality prediction in discrete manufacturing (e.g. same product, two factories). The soft-sensor evidence is from process industry and largely pre-2023.
- The exact per-scenario accuracies of the PHME leakage-safe benchmark could not be read (host blocked).
- No rigorous study was found giving a general "N labelled target samples recovers X%" rule; reported figures are method- and dataset-specific.

## 2. Time-series foundation models and industrial foundation models

### Takeaway
On public forecasting leaderboards the latest TSFMs (Chronos-2, TimesFM-2.5, TiRex, Moirai 2.0) clearly beat naive baselines zero-shot, but a line of 2025–2026 critiques shows well-tuned simple or small dedicated models match or beat them, that performance depends on how close the target is to the pretraining data, and that fine-tuning can hurt on short series. Evidence on genuinely industrial sensor data is thin, mixed, and the positive industrial claims are mostly vendor-reported.

### Cited Findings
- Chronos-2 paper (Oct 2025): on GIFT-Eval, Chronos-2 reports 79.8% average win rate and 46.6% skill score (WQL), ahead of TiRex (70.4%) and TimesFM-2.5 (70.0%); Moirai-2.0 56.0%; Seasonal Naive 10.1%. Self-reported; the authors say the pretraining corpus avoided GIFT-Eval test portions but partly overlaps training portions. In the paper's capability table Chronos-2 is the only model supporting past and known covariates, categorical covariates and cross-learning together; the others are listed as univariate-only — [Chronos-2: From Univariate to Universal Forecasting, arXiv 2510.15821 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.15821)
- Moirai 2.0's own paper places it 5th and 6th on GIFT-Eval by MASE and CRPS (self-reported) — [Moirai 2.0: When Less Is More for Time Series Forecasting, arXiv 2511.11698 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.11698)
- Rankings shift with the benchmark: an independent FUTURE-TS run (June 2026) ranks Chronos-2 fifth overall behind two Toto-2.0 variants, TiRex and Moirai-2.0-R-small, with TimesFM-2.5 seventh — [FUTURE-TS empirical paper](https://tsfm-ai-future-ts.static.hf.space/paper/future_ts_empirical.pdf)
- Critique, NeurIPS 2025: simple baselines such as Seasonal Naive and DLinear set a low bar; carefully tuned plain linear regression beat many deep models and Chronos Base, suggesting current benchmarks may hide real progress — [Linear Regression as a Litmus Test for Time Series Forecasting Benchmarks, NeurIPS 2025](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/130437)
- Critique, arXiv 2411.02796 (described in the search result as ICLR 2025): tuned linear autoregression matched or beat every open-source time-series FM on a standard suite; new FMs are rarely compared against strong, well-tuned baselines — [arXiv 2411.02796](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.02796v2)
- Critique, "How Foundational are Foundation Models for Time Series Forecasting?" (Oct 2025): on data outside pretraining corpora, zero-shot performance depends heavily on alignment between pretraining and target domains; on a proprietary electricity dataset a small dedicated network beat a fine-tuned TSFM — [arXiv 2510.00742](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.00742v3)
- TempusBench (NeurIPS 2025 workshop): existing evaluation frameworks suffer from contamination with pretraining corpora, inconsistent hyperparameter tuning across models, and outdated datasets such as M3 — [TempusBench, NeurIPS 2025](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/130468)
- Break-even analysis (KIT, Jul 2026; Chronos, Moirai, Lag-Llama zero-shot and LoRA vs Naive, ETS, ARIMA, XGBoost; 30 datasets; six training-set sizes from 2% to 100%): FMs win at every training fraction on 15 of 30 datasets; on 6 datasets classical methods beat zero-shot FMs with as little as 2% of training data; on the other 9 the break-even falls between 24 and 8,361 samples. LoRA fine-tuning can actively degrade performance on short series. Proposed rule: if n_train < 700 and seasonality is non-negligible, use the FM zero-shot and skip fine-tuning. Authors call these testable hypotheses, not a validated tool — [When Do Foundation Models Pay Off? arXiv 2607.04919 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04919)
- Industrial multivariate anomaly detection: a 2026 arXiv paper says zero-shot use of foundation models on industrial data "remains largely unexplored" and finds change-point detection the more suitable use, because forecasting error peaks at anomaly boundaries even zero-shot — [Exploring Zero-Shot Foundation Models for Multivariate Time Series Anomaly Detection, arXiv 2607.12454 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.12454)
- HVAC equipment study (2026): foundation models "face challenges in practical anomaly detection scenarios"; the authors instead pair IBM Granite TinyTimeMixer embeddings with gradient boosting and report 88–94% detection rates — [Hybrid Feature Learning with Time Series Embeddings, arXiv 2602.15089 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.15089)
- Manufacturing-specific comparison exists: Calisir and Leszek (TU Berlin), ESANN 2026, evaluate three open-source TSFMs on two manufacturing datasets, zero-shot vs full fine-tuning vs linear probing. Results not retrievable in this session — [ESANN 2026 paper ES2026-310](https://www.esann.org/sites/default/files/proceedings/2026/ES2026-310.pdf)
- A PHM Society paper compares LLMs, TSFMs and classical deep learning for anomaly detection in multivariate industrial signals; only metadata retrieved — [Anomaly Detection in Multivariate Industrial Signals: LLMs, TSFMs, or Classical Deep Learning](https://papers.phmsociety.org/index.php/phme/article/view/5026)
- Industrial pretraining corpora are only now being built: FactoryNet is introduced in 2026 as a pretraining corpus for industrial time series — [arXiv 2605.09081 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09081)
- Siemens **[vendor]**: describes a time-series foundation model built by Senseye Predictive Maintenance that interprets arbitrary sensor data with little extra training; no figures are given. Siemens defines "industrial foundation model" as an umbrella term for specialising models on industrial data not widely available online — [Siemens blog, "The need for foundation models in industry"](https://blogs.sw.siemens.com/thought-leadership/the-need-for-foundation-models-in-industry/)
- Siemens AG research paper (2025, revised 2026, ESANN 2026 preprint): a 750-million-parameter time-series foundation model classifies motor-bearing health from vibration data via in-context examples without fine-tuning. Accuracy figures not retrieved — [arXiv 2511.15447](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.15447v2)

### Inferences
- Zero-shot TSFMs are a credible replacement for a hand-built forecaster when the site has little history (hundreds of points) and the signal resembles public data; they are not a substitute for site data once a plant has thousands of clean, relevant samples or when exogenous drivers matter.
- Public leaderboard gains are measured mainly against Seasonal Naive on univariate public data; this does not establish superiority on multivariate plant sensor data, which the industrial papers describe as "largely unexplored".
- The reusable asset emerging in industry is the pretrained representation (embeddings plus a small site-specific head such as gradient boosting), not an end-to-end model used as-is.

### Gaps
- No independent benchmark of TimeGPT or Lag-Llama on industrial data was found in this session.
- Results tables of the TU Berlin ESANN 2026 manufacturing comparison and the PHME LLM/TSFM/deep-learning comparison were not accessible; these are the two most on-point sources and should be read before citing a verdict.
- No public quantitative evidence was found for Siemens' or other vendors' industrial foundation models beyond their own descriptions.

## 3. Vision foundation models, anomaly detection, and LLM / VLM / agent use (copilots, PLC code)

### Takeaway
Classic inspection benchmarks are saturated (98–99% image AUROC on MVTec AD) while newer, more realistic ones show large drops (about 90% on Real-IAD; below 60% AU-PRO on MVTec AD 2), so benchmark success overstates field readiness. Zero-shot CLIP-based detectors and general VLMs are usable as a starting point but fall short of industrial requirements (GPT-4o 74.9% on MMAD); general LLMs generate vendor-neutral Structured Text reasonably well but fail on vendor dialects unless wrapped in retrieval, compiler and verification loops.

### Cited Findings
- Saturation: the MVTec AD 2 paper (Mar 2025; published in IJCV 134(4), 2026) states that performance on MVTec AD and VisA has started to saturate in segmentation AU-PRO. MVTec AD 2 adds eight scenarios and more than 8,000 high-resolution images with a private test set on an evaluation server; state-of-the-art methods remain below 60% AU-PRO at FPR 0.3 — [The MVTec AD 2 Dataset, arXiv 2503.21622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21622v1); [MVTec dataset page](https://www.mvtec.com/company/research/datasets/mvtec-ad-2). A figure of AU-PRO(0.05) below 31% at 256×256 comes from a review page **[secondary]** — [themoonlight.io review](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/the-mvtec-ad-2-dataset-advanced-scenarios-for-unsupervised-anomaly-detection)
- Real-IAD (CVPR 2024): 150K high-resolution images of 30 objects; most methods achieve about 98–99% I-AUROC on MVTec but only about 90% on Real-IAD — [Real-IAD, CVPR 2024](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Wang_Real-IAD_A_Real-World_Multi-View_Dataset_for_Benchmarking_Versatile_Industrial_Anomaly_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf)
- Real-IAD Variety: 198,950 images across 160 categories; state-of-the-art multi-class unsupervised methods degrade by 10–20% when scaled to this many categories — [arXiv 2511.00540 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00540v2)
- Zero-shot baseline, WinCLIP (CVPR 2023): zero-shot VisA AUROC 78.1% (classification) and 79.6% (segmentation); with one normal reference image WinCLIP+ reaches 83.8% and 96.4% on VisA. On MVTec AD one-shot classification WinCLIP+ scores 93.1% vs 83.4% for PatchCore given the same single image — [WinCLIP, arXiv 2303.14814 summary](https://hyper.ai/en/papers/2303.14814); [WinCLIP supplemental, CVPR 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/supplemental/Jeong_WinCLIP_Zero-Few-Shot_Anomaly_CVPR_2023_supplemental.pdf)
- AnomalyCLIP zero-shot VisA: about 82.1% classification and 95.5% segmentation AUROC, as tabulated in a later paper **[secondary]** — [AnomalyCLIP, arXiv 2310.18961](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.18961); [PA-CLIP, arXiv 2503.01292](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.01292)
- A zero-shot method paper states that full-shot and few-shot anomaly detection are relatively mature and that full- and few-shot methods generally beat most zero-shot methods — [FSLC, SJTU](https://cvl.sjtu.edu.cn/Upload/Paper/FSLC%20Fast%20Scoring%20with%20Learnable%20Coreset%20for%20Zero-shot%20Industrial%20Anomaly%20Detection.pdf)
- Domain shift is recognised as a distinct failure source for foundation-feature inspection: a 2026 preprint (ShiftSplit-AD) is devoted to separating domain shift from defects in DINOv2-feature anomaly detection — [arXiv 2608.27610 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27610). A QIRT 2026 paper on continual learning with DINOv2 for sensing-hardware changes reports retaining over 90% of baseline performance on public and proprietary industrial datasets — [QIRT 2026 full paper](https://qirt2026.unina.it/event/1/contributions/90/attachments/39/129/Full_Paper_90_RAHOU.pdf)
- VLMs: MMAD (ICLR 2025) — 8,366 samples, 38 product classes, 4 public datasets, 39,672 multiple-choice questions in 7 subtasks. GPT-4o averaged 74.9% accuracy, which the authors judge "falls far short of industrial requirements"; models do well on object-related questions and poorly on defect-related ones; the one anomaly-specific model (AnomalyGPT) scored lowest overall because it could not handle subtasks outside its training — [MMAD, arXiv 2410.09453](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09453v3)
- PLC code generation: AutoPLC builds a 914-pair requirement-to-code benchmark for CODESYS ST and Siemens SCL. A general model (Claude 3.5) alone achieves only 27% compilation pass on Siemens TIA Portal and 58% on CODESYS; the AutoPLC vendor-aware pipeline exceeds 90% compilation pass at about $0.13 per task and reaches 78% formal-verification pass on the 23-task Agents4PLC benchmark — [AutoPLC, arXiv 2412.02410](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.02410v2)
- Agents4PLC (2024): multi-agent generate-and-verify system with a benchmark from natural-language requirements to formal specifications; GPT-4 was the strongest backbone — [arXiv 2410.14209](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.14209v1)
- SemaPLC (2026): verification-gated agent checking compilation, specification and live runtime; 72.6% mean strict verified pass rate across seven backbone models on 117 function tasks and 65 project-level tasks — [arXiv 2608.18565 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.18565)
- Early baseline (2023): GPT-4 produced syntactically correct IEC 61131-3 Structured Text in many cases, from a prompt collection rather than a formal benchmark — [ChatGPT for PLC/DCS Control Logic Generation, arXiv 2305.15809](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.15809)
- Siemens Industrial Copilot **[vendor]**: a 2025 Siemens presentation lists engineering productivity and reduced manufacturing-engineering time as goals of the copilot approach but gives no measured results — [Siemens, Scaling Industrial AI (2025)](https://plattformindustrie40.at/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Siemens_ScalingIndustrialAI.pdf)

### Inferences
- What is reusable as-is: pretrained visual backbones (CLIP, DINOv2) as feature extractors, and general LLMs for vendor-neutral code and text. What is not: the decision layer. A handful of normal images from the actual line (few-shot) closes much of the gap zero-shot leaves, and vendor toolchains, compilers and verification must be added around the LLM.
- The 27% vs 90%+ gap on Siemens SCL is a direct measure of how much site- and vendor-specific context matters even for language models: the model is general, the working system is not.
- The saturation of MVTec AD means vendor claims quoted on that benchmark should be discounted; Real-IAD and MVTec AD 2 figures are closer to field difficulty (lighting change, transparent and reflective parts, many categories).

### Gaps
- No controlled study was found that measures the drop when the same inspection model is moved to a different line, camera or lighting set-up in a real factory.
- Full-shot PatchCore on VisA was not verified, so the exact zero-shot vs full-shot gap is unquantified here.
- No independent measurement of copilot productivity in industrial engineering was found; all such numbers located were vendor statements.

## 4. Robotics / physical AI foundation models (factory relevance only)

### Takeaway
Cross-embodiment pretraining demonstrably helps (RT-1-X +50% mean success; RT-2-X about 3x on skills transferred from another robot), but every factory-relevant source still requires task- and site-specific demonstrations: around 100 teleoperated episodes in NVIDIA's workflow and about 10 hours on site for a single packaging task in a Siemens case study.

### Cited Findings
- Open X-Embodiment (Oct 2023): 1M+ real trajectories across 22 robot embodiments. RT-1-X mean success rate is 50% higher than the original per-lab methods. RT-2-X outperforms RT-2 by about 3x on "emergent skills" present only in another robot's (WidowX/Bridge) data; removing the Bridge data significantly reduces hold-out performance, indicating genuine cross-robot transfer — [Open X-Embodiment, arXiv 2310.08864](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.08864); [Google DeepMind blog, 3 Oct 2023](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/scaling-up-learning-across-many-different-robot-types/)
- OpenVLA (2024): 7B-parameter open model trained on 970k real robot demonstrations; outperforms the 55B RT-2-X by 16.5 percentage points absolute success across 29 tasks — [OpenVLA, arXiv 2406.09246](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.09246v3)
- Limits of generalisation: a 2025 study fine-tuned OpenVLA and pi0 on 3,600 demonstrations from 18 seen tasks and evaluated on 23 unseen tasks to probe cross-task generalisation limits — [Exploring the Limits of Vision-Language-Action Manipulations in Cross-task Generalization, arXiv 2505.15660](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.15660)
- Factory-floor case study (Siemens, May 2026): adapting a pretrained pi0.5 policy to one packaging task (pick a transparent accessory bag from a cluttered pile, insert into a cardboard package) required 2,535 task segments, about 10 hours of on-site interaction, in an iterative loop of rollouts, failure analysis, targeted data collection, curation and fine-tuning within a deployment window of about two weeks — [A Factory-Floor Deployment Case Study of VLA Pipelines for Industrial Packaging Task, arXiv 2605.27461](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27461)
- NVIDIA GR00T **[vendor]**: the typical deployment workflow includes collecting at least 100 valid teleoperated episodes; N1.7 is pretrained on 20K hours of human egocentric video plus robot demonstrations, and is the first generation under a commercial (Apache 2.0) licence, the original N1 being non-commercial — [NVIDIA Isaac GR00T developer page](https://developer.nvidia.com/isaac/gr00t/llm-info.md); [GR00T N1.7 on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/gr00t-n1-7)
- NVIDIA **[vendor]**: mixing synthetic with real data gave a 40% performance boost over real data alone for GR00T N1 — [NVIDIA developer blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/accelerate-generalist-humanoid-robot-development-with-nvidia-isaac-gr00t-n1)

### Inferences
- Robotics follows the same pattern as the other modalities: the pretrained model reduces, but does not remove, site data collection. The unit of site effort has moved from "train from scratch" to "hours of on-site teleoperation plus iterative failure-driven data curation per task".
- The Siemens case study suggests the scarce resource in a factory is the deployment window and curation skill rather than compute or model access.

### Gaps
- Success rates before and after on-site adaptation in the Siemens pi0.5 study were not retrievable.
- No independent (non-vendor) data-efficiency comparison of pi0/pi0.5 vs GR00T on a new embodiment was found.
- Secondary guides claim 50–200 or 100–200 demonstrations suffice for a new task and that embodiments with fewer than 5K demonstrations in OXE need more fine-tuning data; these come from vendor blogs and were not verified against primary papers, so they are excluded from the findings.
- I recall the OpenVLA paper reporting fine-tuning with roughly 10–150 demonstrations and the pi0 paper using roughly 1–20 hours of fine-tuning data per task; neither was confirmed in this session and both must be checked before use.

## 5. Data-side bottlenecks and the frequently misquoted statistics

### Takeaway
Reviews agree that failure-label scarcity, class imbalance, heterogeneous sensors and missing standard benchmarks are the lasting obstacles to moving PdM from pilot to plant. The headline failure statistics are weaker than their circulation suggests: RAND's "80%" is a cited estimate rather than its own finding, MIT NANDA's "95%" has been criticised for opaque methodology, Gartner's "30%" is a forecast, and the "80% of time on data preparation" figure is not supported by recent surveys (about 39–45%).

### Cited Findings
- PdM reviews, 2024–2025: data scarcity, imbalance from few failure instances and temporal dependence are named as core hurdles — [Scientific Reports 2024 (PMC11053123)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11053123). Severe imbalance, data heterogeneity and validation under real operating constraints are lasting obstacles to moving from pilots to plant-level use — [IJPHM paper](https://www.papers.phmsociety.org/index.php/ijphm/article/download/4659/2823). Most studies report technical performance with little guidance on integrating results into maintenance workflows — [PeerJ Computer Science 2025, cs-2999](https://peerj.com/articles/cs-2999). Sensors log normal operation far more than failures, pushing failures into a minority class — [Discover Applied Sciences 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42452-025-06827-3)
- RAND (Ryseff, De Bruhl, Newberry; 13 Aug 2024): the report's wording is "by some estimates, more than 80 percent of AI projects fail — twice the rate of failure for information technology projects that do not involve AI". The 80% is a cited estimate, not measured by RAND. RAND's own method was interviews with 65 experienced data scientists and engineers; the leading root causes include stakeholders misunderstanding what problem AI should solve and organisations lacking the data needed to train a model — [RAND RR-A2680-1](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA2680-1.html)
- MIT NANDA, "The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025" (Jul 2025): the "95%" claim concerns organisations seeing no measurable return from generative AI initiatives; reported basis is 300+ public deployments reviewed, 150 leader interviews and 350 employee survey responses. Critics (Wharton's Kevin Werbach; Futuriom; The Information) cite unclear survey data, limited transparency on methodology and sample, and anonymised firms. The same report says external partnerships succeed about 67% of the time vs 33% for internal builds. Coverage is general enterprise GenAI, not manufacturing — [GovInfoSecurity, "Most AI pilots never take flight"](https://www.govinfosecurity.com/most-ai-pilots-never-take-flight-a-29361); [Fortune via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/mit-report-95-generative-ai-105412686.html)
- Gartner (press release 29 Jul 2024): at least 30% of generative AI projects will be abandoned after proof of concept by end of 2025, due to poor data quality, inadequate risk controls, escalating costs or unclear business value. This is a prediction, not a measured outcome, and covers GenAI generally — [Gartner newsroom](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-07-29-gartner-predicts-30-percent-of-generative-ai-projects-will-be-abandoned-after-proof-of-concept-by-end-of-2025.)
- "80% of time on data preparation": no Anaconda survey supports it. Anaconda 2020 reported about 45% of time on data preparation and cleansing, and 2021 about 39% (both via trade-press coverage **[secondary]**) — [BigDATAwire on Anaconda 2021](https://www.hpcwire.com/bigdatawire/2021/07/27/anacondas-2021-state-of-data-science-report-highlights-support-for-open-source-impacts-from-covid-19/); [BigDATAwire on Anaconda 2020](https://www.hpcwire.com/bigdatawire/2020/07/06/data-prep-still-dominates-data-scientists-time-survey-finds/). The 80% traces to older estimates: CrowdFlower 2016 (60% cleaning and organising plus 19% collecting) and a 2014 New York Times "50 to 80 percent" range from interviews **[older]** — [Leigh Dodds, "Do data scientists spend 80% of their time cleaning data? Turns out, no"](https://blog.ldodds.com/2020/01/31/do-data-scientists-spend-80-of-their-time-cleaning-data-turns-out-no/)
- "Pilot purgatory" originates in McKinsey's 2018 Digital Manufacturing Global Expert Survey **[older]**, defined as significant activity underway without meaningful bottom-line benefits — [McKinsey, "How digital manufacturing can escape pilot purgatory"](https://www.mckinsey.com/business-functions/operations/our-insights/how-digital-manufacturing-can-escape-pilot-purgatory)
- Deloitte 2025 Smart Manufacturing survey: only 29% of manufacturers have deployed AI and machine learning at scale across facilities **[secondary: reported via trade press]** — [Manufacturing Digital](https://manufacturingdigital.com/news/the-industrial-ai-barriers-manufacturers-are-facing)
- WEF Global Lighthouse Network, January 2025 cohort: 77% of the top five use cases were enabled by analytical AI and 9% used generative AI — [WEF press release, Jan 2025](https://www.weforum.org/press/2025/01/global-lighthouse-network-2025-world-economic-forum-recognizes-companies-transforming-manufacturing-through-innovation/)
- McKinsey on Lighthouses: fewer than 20% of top use cases in the first three membership waves relied on AI, against nearly 60% for the 21 newest Lighthouses (excerpt truncated; verify wording) — [McKinsey, "How manufacturing's Lighthouses are capturing the full value of AI"](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/operations/our-insights/how-manufacturings-lighthouses-are-capturing-the-full-value-of-ai)

### Inferences
- The best-sourced statement on failure rates is qualitative: RAND's interview evidence that missing or unsuitable training data is among the top root causes. The percentages (80%, 95%, 30%) should be attributed precisely and never presented as measured manufacturing failure rates.
- The technical literature in sections 1–3 and the survey literature point the same way: the binding constraint is site data (labels of rare failures, consistent sensors, context), which is exactly what a model built elsewhere cannot supply.
- Lighthouse AI use is still dominated by analytical (purpose-built) AI rather than generative or foundation models, which fits the technical finding that site-specific models remain the default.

### Gaps
- No original survey was found that quantifies the share of manufacturing AI project effort spent on data; the commonly quoted shares are general data-science figures.
- The exact percentage attached to McKinsey's 2018 "pilot purgatory" finding was not retrieved. A "67% stuck in pilot purgatory" figure attributed to McKinsey 2025 appears only on a blog, and another outlet gives 63% "still in the pilot phase"; both unverified, excluded.
- BCG's 2024 figures on companies struggling to scale AI value were searched for and not found; do not cite from memory.
- Secondary coverage of the RAND report adds a "70–85% data quality" share and a "42% abandonment in 2025" figure that do not appear in the RAND material retrieved; treat as unverified.
- No quantified evidence was found on unit or measurement inconsistency or sampling-rate mismatch as a share of project effort; the evidence is qualitative (reviews, and the bearing paper attributing poor PU transfer to sampling-rate and taxonomy differences).

## 6. Counter-evidence: where pretrained models with few-shot adaptation beat bespoke models

### Takeaway
There is solid evidence that pretraining wins in the low-data regime: with one reference image a CLIP-based detector beats a bespoke one by about 10 points, zero-shot TSFMs beat classical models at every data size on half of 30 datasets, and cross-robot pretraining lifts success by 50%. The advantage narrows or reverses as site data accumulates, and several of the strongest industrial claims lack independently verified numbers.

### Cited Findings
- Vision, one-shot: WinCLIP+ 93.1% vs PatchCore 83.4% AUROC on MVTec AD with a single normal image; WinCLIP+ stays ahead at 2 and 4 shots — [WinCLIP supplemental, CVPR 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/supplemental/Jeong_WinCLIP_Zero-Few-Shot_Anomaly_CVPR_2023_supplemental.pdf)
- Time series: foundation models win at every training fraction on 15 of 30 datasets, and zero-shot use is recommended below about 700 training samples with seasonality — [When Do Foundation Models Pay Off? arXiv 2607.04919 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04919)
- Time series: a July 2025 benchmark preprint reports that foundation models often outperform traditional methods with minimal dataset-specific tuning across zero-, few- and full-shot settings — [preprints.org 202507.0279](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202507.0279)
- Fault diagnosis: BearLLM (AAAI 2025) uses one set of pretrained weights for several bearing tasks and reports state-of-the-art results on nine public benchmarks, beating methods built for individual datasets — [BearLLM, AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/34188/36343)
- Fault diagnosis: UniFault, a bearing foundation model pretrained on over 9 billion data points with few-shot fine-tuning, is claimed to beat task-specific models on real industrial datasets **[secondary: aggregator summary]** — [UniFault listing](https://newx.sg/paper/detail/29cbffa0-f823-11f0-b84c-00163e10baa7)
- Fault diagnosis: LoRA-style few-shot fine-tuning of time-series foundation models is proposed for motor condition monitoring on the argument that task-specific models need large labelled sets and retraining per machine; figures not retrieved — [Data-Efficient Motor Condition Monitoring with Time Series Foundation Models, arXiv 2511.23177 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.23177v1)
- Fault diagnosis: Syn-Diag reports gains over baselines in 1-, 3-, 5- and 7-shot and cross-condition settings; its edge version cuts model size by 83% and latency by 50% — [arXiv 2510.05733 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.05733v1)
- Cross-dataset with limited labels: 92.61% accuracy with 10% labelled target data — [arXiv 2606.24459 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.24459)
- Robotics: RT-1-X +50% mean success from pooled cross-embodiment data; RT-2-X about 3x on transferred skills — [Open X-Embodiment, arXiv 2310.08864](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.08864)
- PLC code: with vendor-aware retrieval and compiler feedback a general LLM goes from 27% to over 90% compilation pass on Siemens SCL without any model training, i.e. reuse plus engineering context rather than a bespoke model — [AutoPLC, arXiv 2412.02410](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.02410v2)

### Inferences
- Synthesis across sections: "reuse and fine-tune" works best where (a) the modality has web-scale pretraining data (images, text, code), (b) site data is scarce, and (c) the task is recognition or generation rather than precise prediction of a plant-specific physical quantity. "Collect site data and build a specific model" still wins where site data is plentiful, sensor configurations are plant-specific, or exogenous process drivers dominate.
- The two approaches are converging in practice: a pretrained backbone plus a small site-specific layer, with site data still required for adaptation, validation and drift monitoring. The honest claim is that foundation models reduce the amount of site data needed and shorten time to a first working model; no source supports the claim that they remove the need for it.
- Much of the positive fault-diagnosis evidence is on the same public laboratory datasets criticised for leakage in section 1, so those results should be weighted below the vision, forecasting and robotics evidence.

### Gaps
- No controlled industrial head-to-head was found that reports labelled-data savings (e.g. "foundation model with N labels equals bespoke model with 10N") on real plant data.
- Performance numbers for the Siemens in-context bearing model, UniFault and the motor-monitoring paper were not retrieved from primary text.
- No evidence was found either way for cross-industry transfer (e.g. a model from semiconductors reused in automotive); the literature evaluates cross-machine, cross-condition and cross-dataset transfer only.
