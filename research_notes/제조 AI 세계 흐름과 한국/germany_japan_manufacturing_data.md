# Germany/EU and Japan: manufacturing data infrastructure, industrial AI and shop-floor workforce (state as of Nov 2026)

Research-method caveat (applies to the whole file): direct page fetches to meti.go.jp, bitkom.org and monoist.itmedia.co.jp were blocked by the session network, so most findings below rest on search-result summaries of the linked pages, not on full-text reading. Figures are given with the reference date stated by the source; anything I could not confirm against a primary page is marked "uncertain". Primary PDFs to re-check are listed in each Gaps section.

## 1. Germany/EU: data standards and data-sharing infrastructures (Catena-X, Manufacturing-X, Factory-X, Gaia-X, AAS, OPC UA, EU Data Act)

### Takeaway
Germany/EU put the data-space and semantics layer first and funded it publicly for years; by 2026 Catena-X is the one piece with measurable operating traffic (about 1,000 data-exchanging participants), while Factory-X only finished its development phase in June 2026 and Mittelstand uptake of data spaces is repeatedly described as sceptical or absent. The EU Data Act has made machine-data access a legal right since 12 Sept 2025, with access-by-design duties for products placed on the market after 12 Sept 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Catena-X: 195 organisations are association members; active participants exchanging data through the network reached 1,069 in September 2026 (headline: "more than 1,000 OEMs and suppliers") — [Automotive Logistics](https://www.automotivelogistics.media/supply-chain/more-thannbsp1000-oems-and-suppliers-now-exchanging-data-through-catena-x-network/2746686)
- Catena-X original project funding is described by Fraunhofer IOSB as over EUR 100 million — [Fraunhofer IOSB](https://iosb.fraunhofer.de/en/projects-and-products/catena-x.html)
- A EUR 23 million "Data Space Accelerator" was launched in 2026 by Catena-X and IDSA to speed SME onboarding; support is tied to companies completing onboarding in 2026; Cofinity-X describes up to EUR 30,000 per SME — [Catena-X](https://catena-x.net/news/e23-million-data-space-accelerator-launched-to-fast-track-sme-integration-within-the-catena-x-ecosystem/); [Cofinity-X](https://www.cofinity-x.com/blog/data-space-accelerator-helps-smes-join-catena-x-with-up-to-eu30-000). (The existence of a dedicated SME subsidy in year five-plus of the programme is itself evidence that SME onboarding was the bottleneck.)
- Catena-X announced a cross-border China–Europe automotive data ecosystem, with operations scheduled to start mid-November 2026 (company statement; not yet verifiable as operating) — [Catena-X](https://catena-x.net/news/catena-x-launches-worlds-first-cross-border-automotive-industry-data-ecosystem-between-china-and-europe/)
- Factory-X (the Manufacturing-X lighthouse for machine/plant builders and factory operators): federal grant no. 13MX001F, project duration 01/2024–06/2026 — [Fraunhofer IOSB](https://www.iosb.fraunhofer.de/en/projects-and-products/factory-x.html)
- Factory-X closing event was held 17–18 June 2026 in Berlin after 2.5 years; the stated result is "the basis for operational use" of an open data space, with open-source components published on the Factory-X GitHub and the Eclipse Tractus-X platform; SMEs are named as the central target group — [Computer&Automation](https://www.computer-automation.de/produktionssoftware/projektabschluss-factory-x-schafft-grundlage-fuer-interoperable-datenraeume-und-industrielle-ki.htm)
- Manufacturing-X committees are in discussion about how to be made permanent so that activity continues after funding ends — [Fraunhofer ISST](https://www.isst.fraunhofer.de/en/departments/industrial-manufacturing/manufacturing-x.html)
- Catena-X and Factory-X interoperability was shown as a showcase/live demonstration at Hannover Messe 2026 (i.e. cross-data-space interoperability is still at demonstrator stage) — [Cofinity-X](https://www.cofinity-x.com/blog/catena-x-meets-factory-x-gec-and-cofinity-x-realize-a-showcase-for-interoperable-data-exchange)
- Criticism, adoption: the industrial Mittelstand is described as "very sceptical to dismissive" toward the "X" initiatives; proAlpha argues that federated data spaces will only spread once standard software ships Manufacturing-X support and working use cases prove benefit — [Computer&Automation (proAlpha)](https://www.computer-automation.de/engineering/proalpha-kehrtwende-in-richtung-digitaler-souveraenitaet.htm)
- Criticism, adoption: at the WGP (German academic production-engineering society) spring meeting in May 2026 it was stressed that many SMEs do not yet use data spaces; WGP called for more speed on shop-floor digitalisation — [Produktion](https://www.produktion.de/wirtschaft/digitalisierung-auf-dem-shopfloor-wgp-fordert-tempo/2664360); [Industrial Production](https://www.industrial-production.de/wirtschaft---unternehmen/shopfloor-fuer-datenraeume-oeffnen--die-wgp-will-kmu-bei-digitalisierung-unterstuetzen.htm)
- Gaia-X: 2026 commentary gives a mixed verdict — a solid regulatory/trust framework exists but market demand is unproven; the framework is called daunting for smaller organisations; Gaia-X's own CTO says "the framework, the software and the criteria are all there... the challenge now is adoption"; sector data spaces are named as the most tangible output — [European Purpose](https://europeanpurpose.com/news/gaia-x-2026-federated-cloud); [EU Perspectives, Jan 2026](https://euperspectives.eu/2026/01/2026-year-of-eu-cloud-sovereignty-trust-needs-framework-insider-insists/); [ITdaily](https://itdaily.com/blogs/cloud/gaia-x-2026-ai/)
- Gaia-X older criticism: bureaucratic, slow decision-making, over-promised — [Cloudflight](https://www.cloudflight.io/en/blog/why-gaia-x-hasnt-been-successful-yet/); a counter-view ("Gaia-X lives – and delivers") — [All About Industries](https://www.all-about-industries.com/european-project-digital-sovereignty-gaia-x-successes-a-5939e22f51d8028e8a2bc17e0b820548/)
- EU Data Act: most provisions apply from 12 September 2025 — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/key-provisions-of-the-eu-data-act-take-effect); the access-by-design duty covers only connected products/services placed on the market after 12 September 2026 — [Fieldfisher](https://www.fieldfisher.com/de-at/insights/data-act-in-kraft-neue-spielregeln-fur-datenzugang)
- Data Act effect on machinery: operators of industrial machines can demand their operational data from the manufacturer (and have it passed to third parties) — [Browne Jacobson](https://www.brownejacobson.com/insights/the-eu-data-act-on-manufacturing-industrial-and-automotive-sectors)
- Data Act pushback: SAP, Siemens, Schneider Electric and DigitalEurope asked for a delay (reported as two years); the Commission refused — [Sigma Software](https://sigma.software/insights/the-eu-data-act-whats-actually-changing-and-what-connected-products-manufacturers-can-do-right-now); [CADE](https://cadeproject.org/?p=151651)
- Data Act enforcement: at the application date some national regulators were reported as ill-equipped to enforce — [Euronews, 12 Sept 2025](https://www.euronews.com/next/2025/09/12/some-national-regulators-ill-equipped-to-enforce-incoming-eu-data-act); the Netherlands set fines of up to EUR 1,030,000 or 10% of turnover (secondary source, uncertain) — [Sigma Software](https://sigma.software/insights/the-eu-data-act-whats-actually-changing-and-what-connected-products-manufacturers-can-do-right-now)
- VDMA's role on the Data Act is implementation-oriented: reported as coordinating 40+ working groups with 700+ companies (figure from trade press, uncertain what exactly is counted) — [Produktion](https://www.produktion.de/wirtschaft/was-der-eu-data-act-fuer-maschinenbauer-bedeutet/1718823)

### Inferences
- The sequencing is unambiguous: semantics/trust/data-space plumbing (2019 Gaia-X, 2021 Catena-X, 2024 Factory-X) preceded any national "industrial foundation model" effort by years. The pay-off so far is narrow: one sector (automotive) with roughly a thousand exchanging participants after about five years and more than EUR 100 million of funding, and regulatory-driven use cases (carbon footprint, traceability) rather than AI training data.
- Membership (195) versus exchanging participants (1,069) shows that the meaningful metric is active connectors, not association members; the two are often conflated in press.
- The repeated need for onboarding subsidies and the WGP/proAlpha statements suggest the binding constraint is integration effort at SMEs and missing support in standard ERP/MES software, not the availability of standards.
- The Data Act changes the bargaining position: machine users no longer depend on voluntary data spaces to obtain machine data. This may reduce the incentive to join data spaces purely for access, while raising the value of common semantics (AAS/OPC UA) so that the released data is usable.

### Gaps
- Asset Administration Shell and OPC UA: I found no 2026 adoption survey, number of standardised AAS submodel templates, or count of OPC UA companion specifications in this session. These need the IDTA (industrialdigitaltwin.org), OPC Foundation and VDMA pages.
- Manufacturing-X total programme budget and the list of sibling projects (e.g. Aerospace-X, Semiconductor-X, Process-X) were not confirmed with a 2026 source.
- No independent evaluation of Factory-X outcomes or of real Mittelstand usage numbers was found; the criticism cited is from associations, vendors and academics, not from a systematic survey.
- No explicit VDMA criticism of the Data Act was found; VDMA position papers (vdma.org) should be checked.
- Whether a Factory-X operating company (analogous to Cofinity-X) exists after June 2026 was not confirmed.

## 2. Germany: industrial AI players, deployment style, and Mittelstand adoption statistics

### Takeaway
Headline AI use in German firms passed 50% in 2026, but shop-floor use is far lower (Bitkom: only about 20% of AI-using industrial firms apply it in production). The large players pursue a domain-specific foundation-model strategy built on pooled proprietary engineering and machine data (Siemens' Industrial Foundation Model and machine-builder data alliance, Bosch's data from 230+ plants), layered on US infrastructure (NVIDIA, Microsoft), rather than reusing generic models unchanged.

### Cited Findings
- ifo business survey (May 2026, published 5 June 2026): 54.5% of German firms use AI in business processes (some press reports 54.4%), up from 40.9% a year earlier; manufacturing 58.7%, services 56.2%, trade about 45%; large firms 67.2%, small 51.2%, medium about 47% — [ifo](https://www.ifo.de/fakten/2026-06-05/mehr-als-die-haelfte-der-unternehmen-nutzt-kuenstliche-intelligenz)
- Bitkom (Sept 2026; telephone survey of 603 companies with 20+ employees): 57% use AI, versus 36% in 2025 and 20% in 2024 — [Bitkom](https://www.bitkom.org/Presse/Presseinformation/Erstmals-nutzt-Mehrheit-Unternehmen-KI)
- Bitkom industry survey ahead of Hannover Messe 2026 (555 industrial companies): 40% already use AI and another 38% plan to — [Bitkom](https://www.bitkom.org/Presse/Presseinformation/Humanoide-Roboter-KI-digitale-Transformation-Industrie)
- Bitkom position paper on scaling industrial AI (Feb 2026): only 20% of AI users apply AI in production (uncertain as to exact base; taken from a search summary of the PDF) — [Bitkom position paper](https://www.bitkom.org/sites/main/files/2026-02/bitkom-positionspapier-industrielle-ki-standort-deutschland-skalieren.pdf)
- Note on conflicting numbers: secondary blogs cite "41%" as a 2026 Bitkom figure; this conflicts with the September release and likely refers to a different survey wave or to the ifo 2025 value of 40.9%. Treat as unreliable — [mybusinessfuture](https://mybusinessfuture.com/bitkom-ki-studie-2026-41-prozent-unternehmen-mittelstand/)
- Siemens Industrial Foundation Model: first presented at Hannover Messe 2025; described by Siemens as a large generative model specifically tuned for engineering and manufacturing — [Siemens blog](https://blogs.sw.siemens.com/nx-manufacturing/teaching-ai-to-speak-the-language-of-engineering-and-manufacturing-through-industrial-foundation-model/)
- Siemens executive Joe Bohman: "AI doesn't know engineering and manufacturing, that's why we're creating this Industrial Foundation Model, which will be built with 150 petabytes of product data and patents" (executive claim, not independently verified) — [Automation World](https://www.automationworld.com/factory/digital-transformation/article/55296419/siemens-digital-industries-software-siemens-bets-big-on-industrial-ais-future-with-altair-acquisition-and-industrial-foundation-model)
- Siemens and machine-tool builders agreed a data alliance (Sept/Oct 2025) to exchange engineering, manufacturing and machine data for generative-AI applications; CEO Roland Busch: "Access to high-quality machine data from different manufacturers is the key here" — [Automation.com](https://www.automation.com/en-us/articles/september-2025/siemens-machine-builders-agree-data-alliance); [CIMdata](https://www.cimdata.com/en/industry-summary-articles/item/28493-siemens-and-machine-builders-agree-on-groundbreaking-data-alliance)
- Siemens builds the model on Microsoft Azure with NVIDIA involvement — [TechCentral/Bloomberg, June 2025](https://techcentral.co.za/siemens-battling-big-tech-ai-factories/265520)
- Siemens–NVIDIA (CES, 6 Jan 2026): expanded partnership to build an "Industrial AI Operating System"; plan for fully AI-driven adaptive manufacturing sites starting in 2026 with the Siemens electronics factory in Erlangen as first blueprint; nine industrial copilots announced; Digital Twin Composer on the Xcelerator Marketplace from mid-2026; PepsiCo named as a user — [Siemens press](https://press.siemens.com/global/en/pressrelease/siemens-unveils-technologies-accelerate-industrial-ai-revolution-ces-2026)
- Siemens Industrial Copilot can be deployed on-premises through Industrial Edge with NVIDIA NIM microservices (secondary source) — [AI Magazine, May 2026](https://publication.aimagazine.com/ai-magazine-may-2026-issue-42/0382693001778579061/p102)
- Bosch: nearly half of Bosch plants use AI in manufacturing; Bosch pilots generative AI/foundation models to produce synthetic data, to be offered to all sites after the pilot (press release date not confirmed) — [Bosch press](https://www.bosch-presse.de/pressportal/de/en/bosch-to-use-generative-ai-in-manufacturing-260806.html)
- Bosch (CES 2026): its learning AI systems draw on data from more than 230 Bosch plants worldwide — [Bosch press](https://www.bosch-presse.de/pressportal/us/en/press-release-31104.html)
- Bosch and Microsoft announced "Manufacturing Co-Intelligence", an agentic-AI offering for production — [Technology Record](https://www.technologyrecord.com/article/bosch-and-microsoft-team-up-to-advance-agentic-ai-in-factories)
- Bosch–Neura Robotics partnership (announced 15 Jan 2026) centres on collecting real-world training and operating data for robots in Bosch factories — [IAA Mobility](https://www.iaa-mobility.com/en/newsroom/news/future-technology/bosch-neura-robotics-humanoid-ai-robots-industry)
- Bosch plans EUR 2.9 billion of AI investment, with manufacturing among the priorities (secondary source, Jan 2026) — [AI News](https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/bosch-e2-9-billion-ai-investment-and-shifting-manufacturing-priorities/)

### Inferences
- German incumbents answer the "fine-tune a general model vs. purpose-built" question with a hybrid: general LLMs from US providers for the language interface, plus proprietary domain data (CAD/PLM, PLC code, machine data) as the differentiator. Siemens' explicit rationale — general AI "doesn't know engineering and manufacturing" — supports the hypothesis that field data matters more than cross-industry model reuse.
- Evidence against a strict "per-site model" reading: Siemens and Bosch are both pooling data across many sites and many machine builders to obtain one reusable model, i.e. they believe transfer across factories works if the data is harmonised. What they reject is transfer from non-industrial data, not model reuse as such.
- Bosch's use of generative models to create synthetic defect images, and the Neura deal focused on data collection, both indicate that scarcity of labelled shop-floor data, not model architecture, is the bottleneck.
- The gap between about 55–59% "use AI" and about 20% "use AI in production" suggests most adoption is office-side generative AI; headline adoption statistics overstate manufacturing AI maturity.

### Gaps
- Release status, pricing and customer count of the Siemens Industrial Foundation Model in 2026, and whether customers fine-tune it per site, were not found. The 150 PB figure is an executive statement.
- Nothing found in this session on SAP (Joule in manufacturing), Aleph Alpha (status after its 2025–26 ownership changes), Fraunhofer model work, VDMA or ZEW adoption surveys. These remain open.
- The Bitkom 20% production figure and the 40% industry figure should be re-read in the original PDFs (bitkom.org was unreachable from this session).
- No quantified ROI or failure-rate data for German shop-floor AI pilots was found.

## 3. Germany: workforce — dual vocational training and qualification for data/AI

### Takeaway
Evidence found is thin: the metal and electrical training occupations are in a revision process led by BIBB, but I could not confirm AI/data content or an entry-into-force date. What is documented is concern from industry associations about skills as a barrier, and employer pressure for faster updating of training regulations.

### Cited Findings
- BIBB has announced a revision ("Neuordnung") of the industrial metal and electrical occupations, listing Industriemechaniker, Anlagenmechaniker, Konstruktionsmechaniker, Werkzeugmechaniker, Zerspanungsmechaniker and Elektroniker für Automatisierungstechnik among others (press release date not confirmed) — [BIBB press release PDF](https://www.bibb.de/dokumente/pdf/PM_neuordnung_metall_und_elektroberufe.pdf)
- IHK Bochum lists these occupations as to be adapted after a BIBB evaluation found further need for change; the entry-into-force date on the page is garbled and cannot be relied on — [IHK Bochum](https://www.ihk.de/bochum/hauptnavigation/bildung/unternehmen/neuordnungen-2026-6746578)
- The 1 August 2026 wave of modernised training occupations is dominated by construction trades and others such as glass process mechanics, not by the core metal/electrical occupations — [BIBB](https://www.bibb.de/neue-berufe2026); [BIBB press](https://www.bibb.de/de/pressemitteilung_216710.php)
- The employers' federation BDA published a 10-point paper (Sept 2025) demanding modern occupations and faster regulation procedures, i.e. the updating process itself is criticised as slow — [BDA](https://arbeitgeber.de/wp-content/uploads/bda-arbeitgeber-positionspapier-10_punkte_fuer_moderne_berufe_und_schnellere_ordnungsverfahren-2025_09.pdf)
- BIBB studied the use of ChatGPT to support the drafting of training regulations (project KINO, 2025) — this concerns how regulations are written, not trainee curricula — [BIBB](https://dspace-test.bibb.de/items/16077e45-34f5-4d5d-94a4-73a34b36d4c9/full)
- WGP (May 2026) links shop-floor data-space opening with support for SMEs in digitalisation, framing know-how at SMEs as the constraint — [Industrial Production](https://www.industrial-production.de/wirtschaft---unternehmen/shopfloor-fuer-datenraeume-oeffnen--die-wgp-will-kmu-bei-digitalisierung-unterstuetzen.htm)

### Inferences
- Germany's formal dual system updates slowly (multi-year social-partner procedure); data/AI competence on the shop floor therefore appears to be delivered mainly through optional add-on qualifications and company/Fraunhofer training rather than through rewritten core curricula. This is an inference from the absence of a completed metal/electrical revision in the 2026 wave, not a sourced statement.

### Gaps
- Not verified in this session: the content of the 2018 partial revision of metal/electrical occupations (digitalisation and data-security module plus optional additional qualifications), take-up rates of those additional qualifications, Fraunhofer Academy / Plattform Industrie 4.0 qualification programmes, and Mittelstand-Digital centres' AI trainers. These are known programme lines that need bibb.de, plattform-i40.de and fraunhofer.de sources.
- No statistic found on the share of German manufacturers citing skills shortage as the main AI barrier in 2026.

## 4. Japan: policy (METI/NEDO, physical AI, Ouranos), genba practice, tacit-knowledge data, players, adoption and criticism

### Takeaway
Japan in 2026 is funding both layers at once but with a clear data-first framing for manufacturing: GENIAC selected nine projects for making manufacturing data "AI-ready" alongside only two robotics-foundation-model projects (May 2026), and the 2026 Monozukuri White Paper diagnoses the core problem as data that is collected but not connected, plus failing skill transfer. In parallel a very large national physical-AI foundation model programme (about JPY 380 billion class) was launched.

### Cited Findings
- 2026 Monozukuri White Paper (26th edition, reported to the Diet 29 May 2026) focuses on AI/digital use and external uncertainty; it states that AI/digital technology is starting to be used for management reform, but knowledge, know-how and talent are constraints, the effect of data use is "somewhat limited" and inter-company data linkage has not spread — [METI white paper summary PDF](https://www.meti.go.jp/report/whitepaper/mono/2026/pdf/gaiyo.pdf)
- Data linkage: about 30% of firms link data within their chain, and 10–20% between chains (MONOist headline reading of the white paper; article title: "Data can be captured but not connected"); the paper calls for data acquisition/use mechanisms designed around business processes — [MONOist, 12 June 2026](https://monoist.itmedia.co.jp/mn/articles/2606/12/news066.html)
- Skill transfer: only about one in three manufacturers say skill transfer is going well (two in three are struggling); 85.5% are anxious about the future — [MONOist, 3 July 2026](https://monoist.itmedia.co.jp/mn/articles/2607/03/news055.html)
- Only 21.7% of firms use digital technology for skill transfer; the most-used tools are digital work instructions and video manuals; among firms with 300+ employees, 65.6% of those using digital tools name AI (image/language recognition, generative AI); the most-cited effect is visualisation/standardisation of the skills to be passed on (77.8%) — [MONOist, 3 July 2026, p.3](https://monoist.itmedia.co.jp/mn/articles/2607/03/news055_3.html)
- Underlying survey: JILPT survey on securing/retaining talent and skill transfer in manufacturing (May 2026), referenced in MHLW material — [MHLW PDF](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/content/11801500/001704747.pdf)
- GENIAC (METI/NEDO), 14 May 2026: nine R&D themes selected on making manufacturing and other data AI-ready and two themes on robotics foundation models — [METI press release](https://www.meti.go.jp/english/press/2026/0514_001.html)
- GENIAC details: 36 proposals were reviewed; AI-ready data projects run for one year within FY2026–27, robot foundation model projects for up to three years (FY2026–29); selected firms include AIONA, iCrystal and SyntheticGestalt — [NEDO list PDF](https://www.nedo.go.jp/content/800054840.pdf); [NEDO call page](https://www.nedo.go.jp/koubo/CD3_100422.html)
- One selected theme (Stockmark) is explicitly about multi-layer structuring of unstructured manufacturing data and converting tacit knowledge into explicit knowledge through an interviewing AI agent, in a joint project with 16 domestic companies — [EnterpriseZine](https://enterprisezine.jp/news/detail/24285)
- Tacit knowledge elicitation: generative AI as interviewer is presented as the answer to the fact that expert interviewing "took a skilled interviewer tens of hours and did not scale" (vendor/blog source) — [Classmethod](https://dev.classmethod.jp/articles/tacit-knowledge-market-2026-update/)
- A separate METI release of 2 July 2026 covers nine selected projects on building data ecosystems (different call from the May one) — [METI](https://www.meti.go.jp/press/2026/07/20260702001/20260702001.html)
- National physical-AI model: METI/NEDO selected Noetra (SoftBank-backed, with NEC, Honda, Sony participation reported) and AIST; budget reported as JPY 387.3 billion in the FY2026 budget (another source: NEDO call up to JPY 383.4 billion — sources conflict); core reasoning model in FY2026, omni-modal model targeted for FY2028 — [SoftBank News, 27 July 2026](https://www.softbank.jp/en/sbnews/entry/20260727_01); [Light Reading](https://www.lightreading.com/ai-machine-learning/noetra-plans-140mw-data-center-to-fuel-japan-s-physical-ai-push); [AI News](https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/japan-ai-robots-2040-national-ai-model/)
- NEDO robotics programme to build a data platform for robot foundation models (data collection, model development, field demonstration) runs 1 Oct 2025 – 31 Aug 2029 — [NEDO PDF](https://www.nedo.go.jp/content/800038898.pdf)
- Ouranos Ecosystem: the NEDO "development and pilot of a data sharing system to realise the Ouranos Ecosystem" continues; in Feb 2026 Circularise joined an ABtC-promoted, NEDO-selected project on battery carbon-footprint data exchange — [Circularise](https://www.circularise.com/press-releases/circularise-joins-nedo-selected-project-to-advance-data-exchange-for-battery-traceability-in-japan)
- Fanuc: partnership with NVIDIA (first reported Dec 2025; Fanuc America release 16 Mar 2026) links ROBOGUIDE with Isaac Sim/Omniverse and Jetson; robots can take voice commands and auto-generate Python code so operators adjust processes without programming skills — [FANUC America](https://www.fanucamerica.com/news-resources/fanuc-america-press-releases/2026/03/16/fanuc-accelerates-physical-ai-in-industrial-robotics-leveraging-nvidia-technologies)
- NVIDIA's Japan partner list also includes Yaskawa, Fujitsu, Hitachi and Kawasaki Heavy Industries — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/nvidia-japan-robotics-smarter-factories-apac/)
- Hitachi survey report "Beyond the Pilot: Industry's Physical AI Progression" (Oct 2026): on average only 16% of respondents report significant AI use by frontline and maintenance teams — [Hitachi](https://www.hitachi.com/en-us/press/hitachi-report-examines-readiness-for-physical-ai-in-industrial-companies/)
- Hitachi (20 May 2026): physical-AI orchestration built around its agent "Naivy", which uses site-specific domain knowledge to coordinate robots and AI systems — [Hitachi](https://www.hitachi.com/en/press/articles/2026/05/0520/)
- Hitachi deploys "Physical AI FDE" (forward-deployed engineer) roles whose task is described as turning veterans' unwritten operating rules into AI-usable data — [Business Insider Japan, Sept 2026](https://www.businessinsider.jp/article/2609-hitachi-tokunaga-physical-ai-4-stages/); [Manufacturing Digital](https://manufacturingdigital.com/news/openai-google-cloud-hitachis-physical-ai-fde-model)
- Hitachi Lumada 3.0 relies on external general models (partnerships with Anthropic for about 290,000 employees, plus OpenAI and Google Cloud for field-specific agents for frontline workers), combined with Hitachi's own operational-technology domain knowledge — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/hitachi-anthropic-lumada-3-claude-enterprise); [ACN Newswire](https://www.acnnewswire.com/press-release/english/103050/hitachi-advances-strategic-alliance-with-google-cloud-to-empower-frontline-workers-with-field-specific-ai-agents)

### Inferences
- Japan's policy mix reveals its diagnosis: nine "AI-ready manufacturing data" projects versus two robot-model projects in the same GENIAC round, and a white paper whose central finding is "data is captured but not connected". Data preparation and tacit-knowledge capture are treated as the scarce input.
- Japan's distinctive element relative to Germany is the explicit targeting of veteran tacit knowledge as a data asset (interviewing agents, FDEs embedded at the genba), driven by demographics. Germany's emphasis is on inter-company semantics and legal data access.
- Contradicting evidence for the hypothesis: Hitachi's architecture reuses general-purpose frontier models (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google) and adds site knowledge on top, and the state is funding one very large cross-industry physical-AI foundation model. So Japan does not reject general models; it bets that the value sits in the site-specific data and people layer wrapped around them.
- Adoption remains low where it counts: 21.7% using digital tools for skill transfer, 16% significant frontline AI use (Hitachi's international sample), 10–30% data linkage.

### Gaps
- No overall "share of Japanese manufacturers using AI" figure from the white paper was obtained; meti.go.jp was unreachable. The data-linkage percentages come from a press headline and should be confirmed in the white paper body.

## 5. Explicit lessons on data quality, semantics harmonisation and shop-floor data burden; evidence for and against the hypothesis

### Takeaway
Both countries' own documents and leading firms locate the bottleneck in data access, harmonisation and human know-how rather than in model capability; however, none of them abandons general foundation models — they wrap or retrain them with pooled domain data. The hypothesis is supported in its "data and people first" half and only partly supported in its "purpose-built beats reuse" half.

### Inferences
- Lesson Germany appears to have learned the hard way: standards and data spaces are necessary but not sufficient; adoption follows a compelling use case (regulatory reporting in Catena-X) and integration into standard software, plus subsidies for SME onboarding. Building infrastructure ahead of demand yielded slow uptake.
- Lesson Japan states explicitly: collecting data is no longer the problem; connecting it, giving it context and capturing human know-how is. Its 2026 funding is directed at exactly that conversion step.
- For a comparison with Korea: both countries treat the "last mile" (semantics, context, tacit knowledge, on-site engineers) as the investment priority, and treat the base model as something to buy, partner for, or build once nationally. Neither is building per-site models from scratch; both aim at pooled-data domain models adapted locally.

### Gaps
- No explicit official statement was found, in either country, on units/measurement harmonisation or on the labour burden of sensor data collection for operators. The closest evidence is the white paper's "captured but not connected" finding and the tacit-knowledge interviewing bottleneck. Treat any stronger claim as unsupported.
- No controlled comparison (fine-tuned general model vs purpose-built model on the same factory task) was found from German or Japanese sources.
