# US federal manufacturing-AI spending: model/platform vs. field workforce (as of 2026-11-13)

Access date for every URL below: 2026-11-13.

**Verification labels used throughout**

- **[P]** = I opened the primary document and read the figure there.
- **[S]** = I saw the figure only in a secondary source or in a search-result summary of a primary document that I could not open. Treat as "확인 필요".
- Sites I could not open in this session: `dol.gov`, `sec.gov`, `dodmantech.mil`, `comptroller.war.gov` / `comptroller.defense.gov` (access denied or not resolvable). Every DOL, 10-K and DoD budget-exhibit figure is therefore [S].

**Classification scheme (rule applied to each line)**

- **A** model development, reuse, fine-tuning, deployment: money whose stated purpose is building or deploying AI tools, models or agents for manufacturing.
- **B** manufacturing data, standards, data infrastructure.
- **C1** shop-floor, incumbent-worker, technician, apprenticeship and domain-engineer training.
- **C2** academic programs (K-12, degree students, faculty).
- **D** compute and physical infrastructure.
- **E** other, including general (non-AI) manufacturing R&D and extension or consulting services.
- A line is given one letter only when the source states its purpose. Where a line mixes purposes and the source gives no internal split, it is marked "mixed, split unknown" and is not apportioned. Judgement calls are flagged "(judgement)".

## Key Question 1. Federal programs and amounts, FY2025–FY2027

### Takeaway
No federal document reports a "manufacturing AI" budget crosscut, so a government-wide share for A/B/C/D cannot be computed. The explicitly AI-in-manufacturing lines are small (tens of millions of dollars a year) and are all model, tool or R&D money (A/B). Workforce money sits in much larger general-purpose programs (DOL apprenticeship, institute workforce projects) that are not manufacturing-AI-specific.

### Inferences
- **Share that can be computed: none at government level.** The table mixes enacted amounts, requests, multi-year ceilings and one-off awards, and most large rows are "mixed, split unknown". Adding them would be misleading.
- **What the known amounts show.** The lines whose stated purpose is AI for manufacturing are rows 3, 4 and 8: roughly $14M a year (planned, unawarded), $20M one-off for two centres, and $8M–$33M a year. All three are A or A/B. I found no federal line whose stated purpose is training shop-floor workers specifically for manufacturing AI.
- Workforce (C1) dollars are larger in absolute terms (DOL rows 14–17 total about $358M in announcements, all [S]) but are general apprenticeship money. Counting them as "manufacturing AI workforce" would overstate the C share; excluding them would give a C share near zero. Either number would be an artefact of the classification choice.
- Row 1 (MEP, $175M) is larger than all explicit manufacturing-AI lines combined, and its existence is contested each year. How it is classified dominates any US ratio.

## Key Question 2. AI Action Plan (July 2025), executive orders and follow-ups

### Takeaway
The AI Action Plan contains no dollar amounts and no headcount targets for manufacturing or workforce; it directs agencies to use existing funding streams and authorities. The only numeric workforce target found is in Executive Order 14278 (April 2025): a plan to "reach and surpass 1 million new active apprentices".

### Cited Findings
- A search of the full plan text for dollar figures and "million/billion" returned nothing relevant; the manufacturing and workforce sections state no amounts or headcounts. — [America's AI Action Plan, July 2025](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Americas-AI-Action-Plan.pdf) [P]
- "Support Next-Generation Manufacturing" has two actions: "Invest in developing and scaling foundational and translational manufacturing technologies via DOD, DOC, DOE, NSF" using SBIR, STTR, research grants, CHIPS R&D programmes, Defense Production Act Title III and other authorities; and convene stakeholders on robotics and drone supply chains. — [AI Action Plan, p. 7](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Americas-AI-Action-Plan.pdf) [P]
- "Empower American Workers in the Age of AI": "prioritize AI skill development as a core objective of relevant education and workforce funding streams", including career and technical education and apprenticeships; Treasury guidance so employers can offer "tax-free reimbursement for AI-related training"; an "AI Workforce Research Hub" under DOL; DOL to "leverage available discretionary funding … to fund rapid retraining". — [AI Action Plan, pp. 6–7](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Americas-AI-Action-Plan.pdf) [P]
- "Train a Skilled Workforce for AI Infrastructure" targets "electricians, advanced HVAC technicians" and similar roles, and calls for expanding Registered Apprenticeships "in occupations critical to AI infrastructure". This is about building data centres and energy infrastructure, not about factory workers using AI. — [AI Action Plan, p. 17](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Americas-AI-Action-Plan.pdf) [P]
- Executive Order 14278, Sec. 4: within 120 days, Labor, Commerce and Education "shall submit … a plan to reach and surpass 1 million new active apprentices", including expansion "to new industries and occupations, including high-growth and emerging sectors". — [Federal Register, Apr 28, 2025](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-04-28/html/2025-07369.htm) [P]
- A CSET article reports "there is still no public plan" for the one-million goal and cites a requested $2.9 billion for the "Make America Skilled Again" consolidation of workforce funding. Article date unclear. — [CSET, "Promises and Progress"](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/promises-and-progress/) [S]
- Genesis Mission (EO 14363, Nov 24, 2025) reportedly tells DOE to identify at least 20 national challenges, a list that includes advanced manufacturing, and sets no spending level. — [Morrison Foerster summary](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251211-executive-order-establishes-genesis-mission) [S]
- The Manufacturing USA report says the network is "poised to support" the administration's "America's Talent Strategy" and the AI Action Plan through "standards for occupations and training for skills needed in emerging technologies". No amount attached. — [Manufacturing USA Report to Congress](https://www.manufacturingusa.com/sites/manufacturingusa.com/files/2026-03/Manufacturing%20USA%20Report%20to%20Congress_2025.pdf) [P]

### Inferences
- US federal strategy treats manufacturing AI as an R&D and procurement matter handled through existing authorities, and treats workforce as an apprenticeship and AI-literacy matter handled through existing DOL and education funding. Neither side has a dedicated budget envelope, which is why no share can be read off a plan document the way it can from a single programme such as M.AX.
- The plan's skilled-trades language concerns AI infrastructure construction. Quoting it as evidence of "manufacturing AI field workforce" investment would be a misreading.

### Gaps
- I did not read EO 14363 itself (downloaded but not parsed) or "America's Talent Strategy".
- No 2026 follow-up document with manufacturing-AI workforce amounts was found.

### Takeaway
This is the one place a share can be computed from a primary source. In FY2023, education and workforce development (EWD) projects were $43.3M of $539.9M total institute expenditure, about 8.0% (FY2022: $40.3M of $453.6M, about 8.9%). - Industry paid only $0.3M of the $43.3M in FY2023. Institute workforce programmes are overwhelmingly publicly funded even though the institutes overall are 70% co-investment.
- These are all-technology figures for 16 institutes (biopharma, photonics, composites and so on), not AI. They indicate how a US public-private manufacturing institute divides money, and are the best available prior for the unawarded AI institute, nothing more.

### Gaps
- The latest published data are FY2023. No FY2024 or FY2025 network report was found.
- No breakdown of the non-EWD 92% into projects, facilities and overhead.

## Structural differences that make a US–Korea (M.AX) comparison misleading

### Takeaway
A US federal "model vs. workforce" ratio is not the same kind of object as a ratio inside one Korean programme budget. The US has no single programme, most deployment spending is private, and the workforce money is generic.

### Cited Findings
- Institutes are 70% co-investment: in FY2023, $160M federal base "catalyz[ed] $380 million (70%) in co-investment". — [Manufacturing USA Report to Congress](https://www.manufacturingusa.com/sites/manufacturingusa.com/files/2026-03/Manufacturing%20USA%20Report%20to%20Congress_2025.pdf) [P]
- The planned AI institute requires non-federal funding "at least that much" as the federal $70M. — [NIST release, Dec 22, 2025](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2025/12/nist-launches-centers-ai-manufacturing-and-critical-infrastructure) [P]
- The AI Action Plan funds manufacturing technology through existing authorities (SBIR, STTR, DPA Title III, Other Transaction Authority) and names no programme budget. — [AI Action Plan](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Americas-AI-Action-Plan.pdf) [P]
- The administration proposed $0 for MEP in both FY2026 and FY2027 while Congress enacted or marked $175M. — [NIST budget update, June 16, 2026](https://www.nist.gov/document/budget-updates-and-discussion-vcat-62026) [P]

### Inferences
1. **No common denominator.** M.AX is one programme with a budget; the US equivalent is scattered across NIST, NSF, DOE, DoD and DOL with no AI-in-manufacturing crosscut. Any US percentage depends on which rows the analyst includes.
2. **Private-led deployment.** Model development and deployment in US factories is mostly paid by vendors and manufacturers and does not appear in federal budgets. A low federal "A" figure does not mean low national spending on models, and a federal ratio says little about the national ratio.
3. **Workforce money is generic.** US C1 funding is apprenticeship and literacy money for all sectors. Counting it inflates the workforce share; excluding it deflates it to near zero.
4. **Request vs. enacted gap.** US figures for FY2026–FY2027 differ sharply between the President's request and congressional action (MEP: $0 vs $175M). A comparison must state which is used.
5. **Reference-year gap.** The only computable US share (institutes, about 8% EWD) is FY2023 and covers all advanced manufacturing, not AI.
6. **Defence weight.** The largest single US manufacturing-technology line found is DoD's ($581.8M FY2027 request, [S]), aimed at defence production; Korea's programme is civilian industrial policy.
